Speed to Lead: What the Research Actually Shows

"Respond fast" is common advice in real estate. Here's the actual research behind it — response time studies from MIT, InsideSales.com, WAV Group, and Real Trends — and what the numbers mean for how you follow up.

Published research consistently shows that contacting a real estate lead within 5 minutes makes an agent roughly 21 times more likely to qualify that lead than waiting 30 minutes, according to the MIT/InsideSales.com Lead Response Management Study — while separate broker research found nearly half of buyer inquiries never receive a response at all.

21x More Likely to Qualify

Agents who contact a lead within 5 minutes are about 21 times more likely to qualify that lead than agents who wait 30 minutes. Source: MIT/InsideSales.com Lead Response Management Study.

48% Never Get a Response

A study of 384 US brokers across 11 states found the average response time to buyer inquiries was over 15 hours, and 48% received no response at all. Source: WAV Group / Weichert responsiveness study.

First Responder Usually Wins

Industry research cited by Icenhower Consulting found that 78% of converted leads went to whichever agent responded first — not necessarily the most experienced one.

Persistence Compounds Speed

Real Trends research found leads who received six or more contact attempts converted at rates roughly 70% higher than leads who received only one or two attempts.

Why the 5-minute window matters so much

The core finding driving most speed-to-lead advice traces back to the MIT/InsideSales.com Lead Response Management Study, one of the most widely cited pieces of research in sales response-time literature. It found that the odds of successfully qualifying a lead fall off sharply as response time increases — the difference between a 5-minute response and a 30-minute response isn't marginal, it's roughly a 21x swing in qualification likelihood.

What makes this especially relevant in real estate is that buyers rarely inquire with only one agent. A lead who fills out a form on a Zillow listing or a Facebook ad is frequently doing the same thing across two or three other listings at the same time. Being first to respond isn't just about speed for its own sake — it's about being the agent still in the conversation when the buyer is ready to act.

The gap between best practice and what actually happens

Separately from the MIT/InsideSales.com findings, a 2014 responsiveness study by WAV Group and Weichert measured actual response behavior across 384 US brokers in 11 states. It found the average response time to a buyer inquiry was over 15 hours — and that 48% of inquiries received no response at all. That gap between the research on what works and what most brokers actually do is where the opportunity sits: any team that closes that gap is outperforming a large share of the market on the single most predictive factor in the data.

Speed gets the contact; persistence gets the conversion

Speed alone isn't the whole story. Real Trends research on buyer lead conversion found that leads receiving six or more contact attempts converted at meaningfully higher rates — roughly 70% higher — than leads that only received one or two attempts. Fast first contact and persistent follow-up are complementary, not competing, strategies.

What this means for how a team follows up

  • The highest-leverage system change most teams can make isn't a new lead source — it's shrinking the time between form submission and first contact attempt
  • After-hours and weekend leads are disproportionately at risk of the 15-hour-average problem, since most teams aren't staffed to respond outside business hours
  • A single fast response isn't enough on its own — pairing speed with multiple follow-up attempts is what the data shows actually converts
